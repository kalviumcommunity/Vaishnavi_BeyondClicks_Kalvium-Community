# Supporting Evidence

## Finding 1: Faster Support Is Associated With Lower Churn

### Evidence
Churn rate by first response time:

| Response Time | Churn Rate |
|---|---:|
| Under 2 hours | 3% |
| 2–4 hours | 5% |
| 4–24 hours | 9% |
| Over 24 hours | 12% |

### Why It Matters

Customers waiting more than 24 hours have approximately four times the churn rate of customers receiving support within two hours.

This gives the business a clear operational target: reduce response time and prevent customer frustration from turning into churn.

---

## Finding 2: Churn Increases as Response Time Increases

### Evidence

The churn rate increases from approximately 3% for customers receiving support within two hours to approximately 12% for customers waiting more than 24 hours.

### Why It Matters

The pattern is consistent with the idea that delayed support creates a poor customer experience. Response time can therefore be monitored as an early warning signal for potential customer retention problems.

---

## Finding 3: Response Time Is Actionable

### Evidence

The difference between 3% churn for customers receiving fast support and 12% churn for customers experiencing the longest delays provides a clear operational gap.

### Why It Matters

Unlike many customer characteristics that the company cannot easily change, support response time can be improved through staffing, prioritization, service-level targets, and monitoring.
