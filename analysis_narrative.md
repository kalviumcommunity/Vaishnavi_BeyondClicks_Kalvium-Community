# Customer Churn Analysis: Executive Summary

## 1. Context — The Business Problem

Customer churn is a major business concern because customers who leave reduce recurring revenue and increase the cost of acquiring replacement customers. This analysis investigates whether customer support response time is connected to customer churn. The goal is to understand whether faster support can help retain customers and identify practical actions that the Operations and Support teams can take to reduce avoidable customer losses.

## 2. Data Summary — What We Examined

We examined customer support interactions, response times, customer subscription information, and renewal or churn status. The analysis focused on the relationship between how quickly customers received their first support response and whether they later churned. Customers were grouped according to their support response time so that churn patterns could be compared across different response-time ranges.

## 3. Key Findings — What the Data Shows

- Customers receiving their first response within 2 hours had a churn rate of approximately 3%.
- Customers receiving a response within 2–4 hours had a churn rate of approximately 5%.
- Customers receiving a response within 4–24 hours had a churn rate of approximately 9%.
- Customers waiting more than 24 hours had a churn rate of approximately 12%.
- Customers waiting more than 24 hours were therefore approximately four times as likely to churn as customers receiving support within 2 hours.

These results show a clear business pattern: slower support is associated with substantially higher customer churn.

## 4. Anomaly Investigation — Why Is This Happening?

The pattern can be explained by the customer experience during a support problem. When customers receive help quickly, their issues can be resolved before frustration increases. When customers wait for many hours or more than a day, they may feel that their problems are not being treated as important. By the time support responds, some customers may already have decided to leave.

The important point is that response time is not simply an operational measurement. It can directly affect how customers experience the company. Faster responses give the support team an opportunity to solve problems before dissatisfaction turns into cancellation.

## 5. Recommendations — What Should We Do?

### Recommendation 1: Hire Additional Support Engineers

- **Action:** Hire two additional support engineers.
- **Why:** Additional support capacity can reduce the current response-time pressure and help the team reach a response target below two hours.
- **Expected impact:** Reducing response times toward the under-two-hour range could substantially reduce churn and potentially recover significant annual revenue.
- **Owner:** VP of Operations and HR.
- **Timeline:** Begin recruitment immediately and target onboarding within the next quarter.

### Recommendation 2: Implement a Two-Hour Response SLA

- **Action:** Introduce a support response-time target of less than two hours for priority customer issues.
- **Why:** The analysis shows substantially lower churn among customers who receive faster responses.
- **Expected impact:** Consistent monitoring should reduce response delays and improve customer retention.
- **Owner:** VP of Operations.
- **Timeline:** Define the SLA within two weeks and begin monitoring immediately afterward.

### Recommendation 3: Prioritize High-Value Customers

- **Action:** Create a priority support queue for high-value customers.
- **Why:** Protecting customers who contribute significant revenue reduces the financial impact of avoidable churn.
- **Expected impact:** Faster support for high-value customers should reduce their exposure to the churn risk associated with long response times.
- **Owner:** CTO and VP of Operations.
- **Timeline:** Design the priority workflow within one month and implement it in the following month.

## Conclusion

The analysis shows a strong relationship between support response time and customer churn. Customers who receive faster support have much lower churn rates, while customers waiting more than 24 hours have approximately four times the churn rate of customers receiving responses within two hours.

The business should therefore treat support response time as an important customer-retention measure. Hiring additional support capacity, introducing a two-hour response target, and prioritizing high-value customers are practical steps that can turn this finding into action.
